# C&C: Napoleonics base game (001–015): rulings

Compiled 2026-11-17 for the browser edition. Scope: the base game only (French, British, Portuguese, and the Allied
brown-block units at 014–015). Every rules question the engine needs settled before `design/rules-reference.md` is
written is decided here. Rules text is paraphrased throughout; the few quotations are under 15 words.

**The decisions table is binding.** `design/rules-reference.md` cites these ids in its `[Interp]` marks, and engine
tests cite them too. A row marked `[Interp]` is our own decision where no ranked source settles the point; its last
column gives the one-sentence reason. A row without `[Interp]` is settled by the source in its basis column.

## Source ranking (binding)

From the plan's Global Constraints. When sources disagree, the higher one wins unless a row explains otherwise.

1. GMT *Rules of Play, 4th Edition* [RB4].
2. The National Unit Reference Cards / Quick Reference Card [QRC] (and [SITE] unit pages, which transcribe the cards).
3. The 2nd-edition scenario booklet [SCN].
4. Richard Borg's rulings in the commandsandcolors.net condensed FAQ [FAQ].
5. BoardGameGeek [BGG].

Other official GMT texts ([GB], the earlier rulebooks and their translations) are used only to date edition changes
and to borrow terminology for scenario objectives; they never override the five ranked sources for the base game.

## Decisions table

Unit and nation codes are the plan's fixed codes (`FR`, `BR`, `PT`, `AL`; `LN`, `LT`, `RI`, `GR`, `YG`, `OG`, `GG`,
`ML`; `LC`, `LNC`, `HC`, `CU`, `GLC`, `GHC`; `FA`, `HA`, `GA`; `LDR`). "b" means the unit's current blocks. "⌈ ⌉" is
round up and "⌊ ⌋" round down. "r1…r5" are ranges in hexes, counting the target hex but not the firer's.

### The spec's open points

| id | point | decision | basis | interp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | French Grenadier fire | `FR/GR` gets **no** extra ranged die: standing fire b, moving fire ⌈b/2⌉. It keeps +1 melee die (attack, battle back, First Strike) and ignores 1 flag. `BR/GR` does get +1 in ranged combat (standing b+1, moving ⌈b/2⌉+1). | Conflict: [RB4] p.9 gives every grenadier +1 ranged die, but the same page says ranged modifiers are on the national cards. Borg email relayed in BGG 1282405 (11 Dec 2014): French grenadiers do not roll the extra ranged die; British do. [FAQ] Units: Spanish and Russian grenadiers likewise lack it. | — |
| R2 | French line +1 melee against infantry | `FR/LN` rolls b+1 in melee when the target is an infantry unit (including a square), whether attacking, battling back or using First Strike, and for its own share of a Combined Arms roll. It rolls b against cavalry, artillery, a lone leader, and in a leader-escape roll. Its fire is unchanged. | [QRC] p.2 ("+1 vs Inf only"; melee 5/4, 4/3, 3/2, 2/1); [SITE] base-game French LN ("Melee and Battle Back … +1 die" against infantry). [RB4] p.11 defers melee modifiers to the national cards; BGG 1224370 confirms it is card-only. | `[Interp]` for leaders only: a lone or escaping leader is not an infantry unit, so the infantry-only bonus cannot apply. |
| R3 | Stacking of terrain reductions when both hexes have terrain | Reductions **add up**: the battling unit's "out of" value for its own hex plus its "into" value for the target's hex, each read for the battling unit's arm and for fire or melee as R43 lists. Within one hex only one terrain set applies: Field Works on a hill replace the hill values when the attack crosses a works hexside (R45). "Field works do not stack" is read as a rule about the target's own hex only, so it never cancels the battling unit's "out of" value: an infantry unit on a fordable river attacking across a works hexside rolls −2 (−1 out of the ford, −1 into the works). The hill entries are relations (uphill, downhill, hill to hill), each counted once. A unit battling back or using First Strike works its reductions as if it were the attacker (its own hex is "out of", the enemy's hex is "into"). Dice never go below 0. | [RB4] p.10 step 6 and p.11 step 6 (the target's terrain "and in some cases" the battling unit's terrain both reduce); p.21 Field Works on hill ("do not stack"); p.12 and p.15 battle back worked "in the same manner as the attacker". [FAQ] Terrain: ford-to-ford, the ford penalties apply. BGG 714345, 1130332, 2661870, 3102524, 1920194 (consensus: cumulative, town-to-town cavalry −6). | `[Interp]` for the scope of "do not stack": [RB4] states it only for works on a hill, inside the Field Works hex's own section, so it is read as limiting the target hex's terrain, not the attacker's hex. |
| R4 | Is Combined Arms artillery spent for the turn? | **Yes.** An artillery unit that joins a Combined Arms combat has made its one battle for the turn, even if its dice are later lost (R40). Each artillery unit can join at most one combat per turn. An ordered artillery unit that has not yet battled may instead support a cavalry unit's bonus melee (R41). | [RB4] p.8 (a unit may only be ordered to battle once per turn; the only exception named is the cavalry bonus melee). [FAQ] Combined Attack: support the first attack with one ordered artillery unit and the bonus attack with a second. BGG 1330479, 2538472. | — |
| R5 | Moving infantry fire and its rounding by nation | An infantry unit that moved 1 hex fires with half its blocks, rounded **up** for `FR` and `BR` and **down** for `PT` and `AL`; then the unit's type bonus (+1 for `LT`, `RI`, `YG`, `OG`, `BR/GR`, `GG`) is added; then cards; then terrain. `BR/LN`'s +1 applies only to standing fire. A light unit that moved 2 hexes cannot fire. Coming out of square, being rallied and being placed by Short Supply are not movement. Where the [QRC] web PDF disagrees with this formula (BR/LT at 1 block shows 1, BR/RI shows 2/2/1, PT/LT at 2 blocks shows 1) the formula wins: BR/LT 1 block = 2, BR/RI 3/2/2, PT/LT 2 blocks = 2. A base moving-fire result of 0 (PT/LN, PT/ML, AL/LN at 1 block) means the unit cannot fire, whatever card it was ordered by (R15). | [RB4] p.9 (halving, rounding by army, +1 types, worked French example). [QRC] pp.1–2 ("–" in those 1-block cells). [FAQ] Superb Infantry Training (Borg, 30 Jul 2015) and Borg on ConsimWorld (3 May 2012, relayed in BGG 1031404): British line lose the +1 when moving. BGG 1031404: the printed Spanish-expansion chart shows BR/LT 2 and BR/RI 3/2/2, and the web chart is acknowledged wrong. [SITE] base-game unit pages (all "1/2 blocks round up/down, +1"). Rallied units are not "moved": BGG 610971 (Borg view relayed). | — |
| R6 | Lone leaders as fire targets | A leader alone in a hex can **never** be targeted by ranged fire. He still obstructs line of sight like any leader (R49), and he can be attacked in melee (R21). The Expansion #5 rule that lets 2+ fire dice hit a lone leader on 2 sabres is not used. | [RB4] p.10 ("may not be targeted in ranged combat"; design note) and p.12. [FAQ] Leaders: the ranged-fire change belongs to the Generals, Marshals & Tacticians rules. | — |

| R14 | Hill batteries firing over adjacent lower friendly units | An artillery unit on a hill hex may fire over a friendly unit or leader on an adjacent hex of lower elevation, provided that adjacent hex has no terrain that blocks line of sight; that one hex is then ignored as an obstruction and the rest of the line is checked normally. Only artillery may do this, only over the adjacent hex, and **never** for Combined Arms (R39). The target may be at any elevation, including another hill across low ground. | [RB4] p.20 (hill text and example) and p.18 (Combined Arms exception). | R15 | Zero base dice, and zero-dice attacks | **Principle:** a unit whose **base** fire dice against a target are 0 cannot fire at that target, and card bonuses never create a fire attack from 0 base dice. Otherwise a unit may declare fire or melee only if its dice for that attack (after cards and terrain, plus any Combined Arms artillery dice) are at least 1. If dice fall to 0 after declaration (a square forms, R31; First Strike losses), the attack still goes ahead with no dice and has no effect, but it still counts as the unit's battle for the turn. A 0-dice melee against a lone leader therefore does not make him retreat. | [RB4] p.9 (a moved HA "cannot fire at 4 hex range"), p.16 and p.18 (a square or cavalry "may not have a die to roll"); p.18 Combined Arms (the melee unit's share may be 0 while the artillery rolls). ### Dice and leaders

| id | point | decision | basis | interp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R16 | Dice faces and what scores | Each die has 2 infantry faces, 1 cavalry, 1 artillery, 1 flag and 1 sabre. **Fire:** a symbol matching the target's arm scores 1 hit; sabres and other symbols miss; flags may force retreats. **Melee:** a matching symbol or a sabre scores 1 hit (except R17); flags may force retreats. Infantry and cavalry roll dice equal to blocks plus their type bonus; artillery uses R11; squares use R31. Excess hits are lost. Hits are removed before retreats. | [RB4] pp.2–3 (faces), p.8 (dice = blocks), p.10 (fire hits), p.11 (melee hits). | — |
| R17 | Sabre hits: melee only, the militia and rifle exceptions, lone leaders | Sabres never score in fire. In melee a sabre scores against any unit, except that a sabre rolled by `ML` or `RI` scores nothing against a unit. Against a lone leader (direct melee, R21, or escape, R24) any sabre eliminates him, even one rolled by `ML` or `RI`. Artillery sabres score in its own melee. Artillery dice in a Combined Arms roll score on sabres unless the melee unit is `ML` or `RI`, in which case they do not. In a Retire and Reform roll (R34) sabres score nothing. Leader casualty checks are made with sabres (R18). | [RB4] p.10 (no sabre hits in fire), p.11 (militia and rifle exception, lone-leader exception), p.12, p.14 (escape), p.16 (R&R), p.19 (Combined Arms sabres); [QRC] ("Sabers Hit" on artillery; "No Saber Hits" on militia and rifles). | — |
| R18 | Leader casualty check, including after a failed retreat | The opponent always rolls. **Unit loses blocks and survives** (from fire, melee or a retreat it could not complete): roll 2 dice; 2 sabres kill the leader. **Unit eliminated:** roll 1 die; 1 sabre kills him; otherwise he must retreat 1–3 hexes (R25), and flags rolled at the unit do not affect him. One combat roll causes at most one check. Order: remove hits, make the check, then resolve retreats. So if hits left the unit alive (2-dice check made) and the failed retreat then destroys it, there is no second check; the surviving leader retreats from the hex where the last block was removed. If the unit took no hits but loses blocks only through a failed retreat, that loss triggers the check: 2 dice if it survives, 1 die if it is eliminated. A leader killed in the check cannot provide his flag-ignore. | [RB4] p.12 (Leader Casualty Check, failure to complete a retreat, one check per roll), p.13 (Bolster Morale: retreats come after losses and the check). BGG 1096966 (the 2-dice-then-eliminated case). | — |
| R19 | What happens to a leader whose unit is destroyed | After surviving the 1-die check, the leader retreats 1–3 hexes. On his own baseline he must leave the map: no banner for the enemy. If enemy units fill every retreat path he must escape (R24). If impassable terrain stops him retreating even 1 hex he is eliminated and the enemy gains a banner. Once he has left the hex, the attacker may take ground or break through into it. | [RB4] p.12 (Attached Leader's Unit Eliminated), p.13 (Leader's Retreat). | — |
| R20 | Leaders give +1 die only on Leadership | An attached leader never adds dice, never adds hits and never enables a bonus melee, except that a unit **ordered by the Leadership card** rolls 1 extra die in each battle it makes that turn (initial melee, fire, or a cavalry bonus melee). It does not apply to battle backs on the opponent's turn, and cannot lift a square or a cavalry unit attacking a square above 1 die (R31). The attached leader always lets the unit ignore 1 flag (R18 caveat). | [RB4] p.23 (Leadership card text), p.13 (leader flag-ignore); [SITE] base-game Leader ("modify the number of dice … when a Leadership card is played"). | `[Interp]` for the bonus melee: Leadership grants the die whenever the ordered unit "battles", and a bonus melee is a battle made that turn. |
| R21 | A lone leader attacked in melee | The attacker rolls its normal melee dice for the hex (terrain and card bonuses apply as in any melee; the `FR/LN` +1 does not, R2). Any sabre kills him; flags have no effect; if he survives he must retreat 1–3 hexes. The attacker may then take ground or break through, and cavalry that breaks through may make its bonus melee. A lone leader can never be fired at (R6). | [RB4] p.12 (Unattached Leader), p.14 (take ground and breakthrough after a lone leader retreats), p.15 (bonus attack after a successful melee; "successful" includes forcing a leader out, p.14). | — |
| R22 | A lone leader counting toward support | A friendly leader alone in an adjacent hex counts as one supporting unit. A unit with 2 or more adjacent supporters (units, squares, lone leaders in any combination) may ignore **one** flag; more supporters never give a second. A unit in square gives support but never receives it. | [RB4] p.13 (Bolster Morale bullets 2–4), p.18 (squares and support). BGG 2941968 (4 supporters still ignore only 1). | — |
| R23 | Bolster Morale sources and order | Flag-ignores add up: attached leader (who must first survive his casualty check if blocks were lost) 1; support 1; Field Works when attacked across a works hexside, for infantry and artillery only (squares included) 1; `GR` and `CU` 1; `YG` 1; `OG`, `GG`, `GHC`, `GA` 2. Ignoring is optional and chosen flag by flag. Bounce flags (R30) can never be ignored. The fortress at 006 adds 1 for Anglo-Portuguese units by scenario rule. | [RB4] p.13; [QRC] "May Ignore" column; [FAQ] Terrain (a square behind works ignores 1 flag; Borg, Sep 2014); [SCN] 006. | — |
| R24 | Leader escape | When enemy units occupy the retreat path a leader must take, he moves onto the enemy hex and that enemy unit rolls its normal melee dice: its own unit-type bonus and formation limits count (a square rolls 1 die), but no terrain and no card bonuses. Any sabre kills him (even from `ML` or `RI`). If he survives he continues; a second enemy-occupied hex means a second escape roll; if a third hex is also enemy-occupied he is eliminated. Each elimination gives the enemy a banner. A hex holding only a lone enemy leader can be passed through with no roll. Escape also applies when the leader's unit lost its last block because enemy units fill its only retreat hexes. | [RB4] p.12 (escape when enemy units fill the path), p.14 (procedure and examples); [FAQ] Leaders and Leader Escape (no terrain or command card effects; heavy cavalry in a town rolls 4; a square rolls 1; a lone enemy leader rolls nothing). | — |

## Source conflicts and how they were ruled

- **French Grenadier fire (R1).** [RB4] p.9 gives all grenadiers +1 ranged; the [QRC], [SITE] and a relayed Borg email
  say French grenadiers do not. Ruled for the national card: [RB4] itself defers ranged modifiers to the cards, and the
  designer confirmed the card.
- **Where the line between R1 and R5 falls.** A national-card value wins over [RB4]'s general text when the card is
  consistent with itself and with the other card sources, and the designer has confirmed it (R1: every French
  grenadier cell, the site transcription and Borg agree). An isolated card cell that contradicts both [RB4]'s formula
  and the card's own analogous rows, and that a printed chart corrects, is a typo and loses to the [RB4] formula (R5).
- **Moving fire values on the web [QRC] (R5).** Three cells (BR/LT at 1 block, the BR/RI row, PT/LT at 2 blocks) contradict
  the [RB4] formula. Ruled for the formula: [RB4] ranks higher, a printed Spanish-expansion chart agrees with it, and the
  same card applies the formula correctly to every analogous row (French LT, BR/GR, GG).
- **Squares on Field Works (R70).** [RB4] bans them outright; Borg's FAQ ruling allows them through an open hexside in
  the core game. Ruled for the FAQ as designer errata. This is the one place a lower-ranked source wins; it is flagged
  for the reviewer.
- **Sand Quarry (R71).** [RB4] differs from every earlier edition and reportedly from the 2025 chart. Ruled for [RB4].
- **Guard foot artillery movement (R13).** [SITE] gives horse-artillery movement; [RB4] and [QRC] give foot. Ruled for
  [RB4].
- **Hougoumont (R72).** Two FAQ entries pull opposite ways. Ruled for the booklet as designed.
- **Exit objectives (R67).** [BGG] is split on a start-of-turn requirement; the booklet's literal wording is followed and
  [GB]'s later definition is not imported.
- **Temporary banners (R66).** [GB] (2025) lets a banner survive if another ordered unit re-enters the hex that turn;
  the booklet says the banner is lost when the unit moves off or is eliminated. Ruled for the booklet ([SCN] outranks an
  expansion text).
