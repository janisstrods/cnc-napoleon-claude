# Battles 101-103 verification (independent, Opus)

Verifier: independent Opus reviewer, 2026-11-17. Files checked (commit e124ecb, branch `smaps-a`): `design/data/101.json`,
`102.json`, `103.json` and `notes-101-103.md`. No data file was edited.

**Result: 101 and 103 match the maps. 102 has 1 discrepancy:** the two printed `Exit->` / `<-Exit` signs (0,0 and
0,12) are recorded as `objectives`, but the map draws no objective hexes there and the scenario rule is an exit off the
whole Spanish edge. Every unit, leader, terrain hex, river and bridge on all three maps is correct. The 18 hills on 101
(survey/page count 15) are all real: each one shows its ring of rays, so following the map is right.

## Method

- **Read the map first.** Each map (`cnc-napoleonics-research/maps/1NN.jpg`) was read hex by hex from its own grid
  crops before the data was compared: every block (colour, code label, block count, stacked leader), every terrain hex,
  river seams, the bridge, town labels and the Exit signs. That reading was then compared with the JSON entry by entry.
- **Grid.** Each file's `grid` equals the map's fit in `scripts/maps/grids.json` (101: 74.38, 129.88, pitch 75.5;
  102: 74.12, 133.38, pitch 75.12; 103: 73.88, 129.88, pitch 75.5; w 87). In the crops the grid lines sit on the
  printed hex outlines and on the white seams between river tiles on all three maps.
- **Renders.** `render.py` drew each file over its map (`map-work/verify-101-103/r101.png`, `r102.png`, `r103.png`).
  At full size every unit tag, leader tag, terrain letter, the bridge bar and the objective outlines sit on the map
  feature they name.
- **Crops.** `crops.py` with grid and r,c labels at 2-3× over the whole board of each map
  (`map-work/verify-101-103/c101a`-`c101f`, `c102a`-`c102d`, `c103a`-`c103c`). Every unit cluster, hill block, forest,
  river, bridge, town and the Exit signs were looked at in these.
- **Leader labels.** The rotated side labels were cropped, turned upright and enlarged 10-14×
  (`lab101.png`, `lab102.png`, `lab103.png`, `lab-doubt.png`, `lab-ray.png`). Label widths were also measured in pixels
  and compared with the widths of the candidate names set in a sans font.
- **Block counts by eye** per side, nation and code on each map, compared with the JSON and the survey matrix.
- **check.py**: `ok` for all three files.
- **scan.py**: 101 flags 3,9 (hill 0.11, data hill); 102 flags 3,3 (water 0.02, data river with bridge); 103 none.
  Both flags are false: 3,9 shows rays above and below the SP/FA in the crop; on 3,3 the bridge art covers the water.
  `scan.py --dump` was read for every hex: on all three maps the data's hills score 0.11-0.20 and every other hex
  0.00-0.06; forests 0.20-0.27 and nothing else above 0.06; water above 0.01 only on the data's river hexes (3,3 at
  0.02 under the bridge). So no hill, forest or river hex is missing or extra.
- **Scenario pages** (commandsandcolors.net, 101-103) read for the exit wording, terrain counts and the commanders
  named in the historical notes.

## 102 Zornoza — 1 discrepancy

- **Units, top (15):** SP/LN 1,5 (Riquelme behind it) 2,4 2,6 2,10 2,11 3,9 4,2 4,7 (Ray behind it) 5,7; SP/GR 1,6 4,1;
  SP/LT 2,9 4,3 4,6; SP/FA 3,2. Lone horse leaders Carbajal on 1,10 (on a hill) and Mendizabal on 3,1. All yellow.
- **Units, bottom (16):** FR/LN 6,10 6,11 (Leval) 6,12 7,0 7,1 (Villatte) 7,2 7,5 (Sebastiani) 7,6 7,7 7,10 8,1 8,2 8,5
  8,7; FR/FA 7,3 8,6.
- **Hill (29):** 0,2 0,6 0,8 1,0 1,1 1,6 1,10 2,6 2,7 2,9 2,10 2,11 3,2 3,8 3,9 3,11 4,1 4,2 5,0 5,11 6,3 6,6 6,9 6,12
  7,2 7,6 7,9 7,11 8,2. The occupied ones (1,6 1,10 2,6 2,9 2,10 2,11 3,2 3,9 4,1 4,2 6,12 7,2 7,6) all show rays; scan
  0.16-0.20 on all 29, 0.00-0.05 elsewhere. Equals the page.
- **Forest (6):** 0,7 3,0 4,0 4,12 5,9 6,7.
- **River (10, one piece):** 0,3 (from the top edge), 1,2, 2,3, 3,3 (bend with the bridge), 3,4, 4,5, 5,4, 6,4, 7,4,
  8,4 (off the bottom edge). On 3,3 the river comes in from 2,3 and turns east into 3,4; the stone bridge is drawn over
  it, so `crossing: bridge` there is right. Equals the page's River 4 + bend 5 + bend with bridge 1. The rest is
  fordable by the page rule; no other crossing recorded.
- **Leaders (7):** "Riquelme", "Carbajal", "Mendizabal", "Leval", "Villatte", "Sebastiani" read upright at 10-14×.
  Carbajal: eight letters, the label width matches the name (0.90 of "Riquelme"'s width, the font gives 0.88); it fits
  history (Carbajal led a division of Blake's Army of Galicia), so the reading stands. The page's notes name only Blake
  and Lefebvre on the battlefield, so they cannot confirm any division name. The 4,7 label ("Ray") is the least clear:
  it is three letters wide (0.43 of "Riquelme", against 0.42 for a three-letter name and 0.58 for "Mahy", the
  historical reserve commander), and the second letter could be "a" or "e". "Ray" is the best reading; it is not
  flagged as a discrepancy.
- **Discrepancy 1 — objectives 0,0 and 0,12.** The map prints small `Exit->` (in 0,0) and `<-Exit` (in 0,12) signs.
  It draws no objective outline on either hex. The page's rule is: "Ordered Spanish units may move off the Spanish edge
  of the battlefield"; after three have moved off, each further one scores a banner, and leaders score nothing. The
  page never names exit hexes; the signs only point along the top edge. In this data format `objectives` are the
  hexes a banner is tied to (001's temporary-banner hills, 003's town pair, 006's single exit hex, all outlined on
  their maps), so the two entries tell Plan 3B that only those two hexes count. A player at 0,6 could not then exit,
  which is wrong. The notes even say Plan 3B should ignore the two hexes, which shows the field is the wrong place.
  **Recommendation:** delete both entries (`"objectives": []`). Keep one note that says the Spanish exit is the whole
  top edge (any ordered Spanish unit on a row-0 hex, 0,0-0,12, moving off the top edge), with the three-free-exits and
  no-score-for-leaders terms from survey 102, and that the `Exit->`/`<-Exit` signs printed in 0,0 and 0,12 are art
  only. The banner rule then lives with the scenario rules (survey 102), not in hex data. If a renderer wants the
  signs, a later format change can add an edge field (for example `"exitEdge": "top"`), not objective hexes.
